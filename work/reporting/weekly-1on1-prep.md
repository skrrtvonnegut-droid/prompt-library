# Weekly 1:1 Prep from Daily Updates

## Purpose

Turn a week of daily work updates, notes, ticket summaries, or activity logs into a focused **1:1 preparation brief** that makes accomplishments visible, surfaces recurring problems, and clarifies priorities for the coming week.

Use this when your daily notes contain plenty of activity but you need to answer the more useful questions:

- What actually moved forward?
- What value did the work create?
- What keeps recurring?
- Where is risk or friction accumulating?
- What needs a manager's awareness or support?
- What should be prioritized next?

The goal is not to produce a chronological replay of the week. It is to turn operational activity into a useful management conversation.

## Prompt

Act as an operations-focused analyst helping me prepare for a weekly 1:1 with my manager.

Analyze the daily updates, work logs, ticket notes, or other source material I provide from the past week.

Transform them into a concise **Weekly 1:1 Prep Brief** focused on:

- meaningful outcomes,
- visible wins,
- recurring operational issues,
- unresolved risks,
- decisions or support needed,
- and priorities for the coming week.

Do not simply summarize each day.

Your job is to identify the story of the week:

> **What became better, safer, clearer, more reliable, or easier because of the work that happened?**

---

## Analysis Instructions

### 1. Translate Activity into Outcomes

Look beyond task descriptions.

For each significant piece of work, determine what outcome it created or supported, such as:

- restored or protected service availability,
- reduced operational risk,
- resolved an access or security issue,
- improved governance or auditability,
- eliminated manual effort,
- improved documentation or knowledge transfer,
- removed a recurring source of friction,
- enabled another team to work,
- reclaimed capacity or licensing,
- improved process reliability,
- or advanced a larger initiative.

Do not overstate impact.

If the source only supports an activity and no meaningful outcome can be established, describe it plainly rather than inventing strategic significance.

---

### 2. Make Wins Visible

Identify accomplishments worth bringing into the 1:1.

Prioritize work that demonstrates:

- meaningful problems solved,
- risks reduced,
- initiatives advanced,
- cross-team coordination,
- process improvements,
- preventative work,
- automation,
- improved service quality,
- or previously invisible operational work that deserves visibility.

Combine related activities into a larger outcome when they represent the same body of work.

Avoid turning every completed ticket into a separate "win."

---

### 3. Identify Recurring Friction

Look across the entire week for patterns rather than treating every issue independently.

Examples include:

- repeated access problems,
- manual administrative work,
- recurring security findings,
- unclear ownership,
- licensing problems,
- fragile processes,
- repeated user confusion,
- recurring tickets,
- dependencies on individual knowledge,
- or work that repeatedly interrupts planned priorities.

For each recurring pattern, explain:

- what is happening,
- what evidence in the week's updates supports the pattern,
- and why it may deserve attention.

Do not call something recurring based on a single occurrence.

---

### 4. Surface Risk and Unfinished Work

Identify items that remain:

- unresolved,
- partially remediated,
- blocked,
- waiting on another person or team,
- dependent on a decision,
- or likely to reappear.

Distinguish between:

- **active risk**,
- **operational follow-up**,
- **watch items**,
- and **normal work still in progress**.

Do not inflate routine unfinished work into risk without evidence.

---

### 5. Identify Manager-Relevant Topics

Surface anything where the 1:1 could provide useful leverage, such as:

- a decision,
- prioritization,
- escalation,
- additional resources,
- stakeholder alignment,
- ownership clarification,
- approval,
- or protection of focus time.

Do not manufacture asks simply to populate the section.

If no managerial intervention is needed, say so.

---

### 6. Establish Next-Week Priorities

Based on unresolved work, recurring patterns, commitments, and emerging opportunities, recommend a small number of priorities for the coming week.

Priorities should be:

- specific,
- achievable,
- connected to an observable outcome,
- and grounded in the supplied updates.

Favor completing meaningful work over producing a long task list.

When useful, distinguish between:

- **Must move**
- **Should move**
- **Can wait**

---

## Output Format

### 1. Week in Brief

Provide a short narrative summary of the week.

Focus on:

- the dominant themes,
- what materially moved forward,
- and the current operational posture.

Keep this to roughly 3–5 sentences.

---

### 2. Wins & Outcomes

Identify the strongest accomplishments from the week.

For each include:

**Outcome**  
What improved or was accomplished.

**Work Behind It**  
The significant activities that produced the outcome.

**Why It Matters**  
The operational, security, governance, customer, or business value supported by the work.

Keep the framing grounded in the source material.

---

### 3. Recurring Issues & Patterns

For each meaningful pattern include:

- **Pattern**
- **Evidence from the week**
- **Operational impact**
- **Potential next step**

If no meaningful recurring pattern is supported by the source, state that rather than forcing one.

---

### 4. Risks / Follow-Ups

Capture unresolved items that should remain visible.

For each include:

- **Issue**
- **Current state**
- **Risk or impact**
- **Next action**
- **Dependency / blocker**, if known

Clearly distinguish unresolved work from completed work.

---

### 5. Manager Discussion Items

Identify topics worth discussing during the 1:1.

For each include:

- **Topic**
- **Why it is worth discussing**
- **Decision, support, or alignment needed**

If the week does not contain a genuine manager-level ask, keep this section minimal.

---

### 6. Priorities for Next Week

Recommend **3–5 priorities maximum**.

For each priority include:

- **Priority**
- **Desired outcome**
- **Why now**
- **Known dependency**, if applicable

Prefer outcomes such as:

> Complete the contractor access review and establish ownership for unresolved records.

over activity-only language such as:

> Work on contractor spreadsheet.

---

### 7. Suggested 1:1 Agenda

Create a compact agenda that can be used directly during the meeting.

Example structure:

1. Wins and meaningful progress
2. Recurring issues or operational friction
3. Risks and unresolved work
4. Decisions / support needed
5. Priorities for next week

Adapt the agenda to the actual week rather than mechanically reproducing this structure.

---

## Interpretation Rules

Before finishing, verify that:

- the output is not simply a chronological summary,
- activity has only been translated into outcomes when supported,
- routine ticket volume has not been mistaken for impact,
- recurring patterns are based on multiple signals,
- completed work is not presented as unresolved,
- unfinished work is not automatically labeled as risk,
- managerial asks are genuine,
- next-week priorities are grounded in the source,
- and important preventative or behind-the-scenes work has not disappeared simply because nothing broke.

Prefer **outcomes over activity** and **signal over volume**.

A useful 1:1 brief should help answer:

> **What did I make better this week, what is still making noise, and where should my attention go next?**
