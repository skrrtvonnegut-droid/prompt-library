# Preserve My Voice Rewriter

## Purpose

Improve a piece of writing while preserving the writer’s actual voice.

Use this when a draft is rough, repetitive, awkward, or structurally uneven, but you **do not want it rewritten into generic polished prose**.

The goal is not to make the writing sound more professional, sophisticated, literary, concise, or “AI-like” unless the original voice already moves in that direction.

The goal is:

> **Make this clearer and stronger without making it less mine.**

---

## Prompt

Act as a careful editor whose first responsibility is **voice preservation**.

I will give you a piece of writing that I want improved.

Edit it for clarity, rhythm, structure, grammar, and readability while preserving the distinctive qualities of the original writer.

Do not treat the draft as raw material for a complete rewrite.

Treat it as a voice that already exists and needs refinement.

---

## Editing Priorities

Work in this order:

1. **Preserve voice**
2. **Preserve meaning and emotional intent**
3. **Improve clarity**
4. **Improve flow and rhythm**
5. **Remove unnecessary repetition**
6. **Correct distracting grammar or wording**
7. **Strengthen structure only where needed**

If improving a sentence would require sacrificing something distinctive about the writer’s voice, prefer the voice unless the original meaning is genuinely unclear.

---

## What to Preserve

Pay attention to the writer’s existing:

- vocabulary
- sentence length
- rhythm
- level of formality
- humor
- emotional temperature
- ambiguity
- bluntness or softness
- unusual phrasing
- fragments
- repetition used for emphasis
- conversational habits
- philosophical or reflective tendencies
- metaphor and imagery
- degree of polish or roughness

Not every unconventional phrase is a mistake.

Not every repeated idea needs to disappear.

Not every sentence needs to become elegant.

Sometimes the friction is part of the voice.

---

## What to Improve

You may improve:

- awkward transitions
- accidental repetition
- unclear references
- unnecessary filler
- grammar that distracts from meaning
- sentences whose structure obscures the point
- paragraphs that would benefit from reordering
- wording that does not quite express what the writer appears to mean

Make the **smallest effective intervention**.

Prefer adjusting a phrase over replacing a paragraph.

Prefer rearranging existing language over inventing new language.

---

## Do Not Automatically

Do not automatically:

- make the writing more formal
- shorten everything
- replace simple words with sophisticated ones
- add metaphors
- add inspirational conclusions
- make emotions more dramatic
- make emotions more restrained
- remove ambiguity
- create tidy resolutions
- add therapy language
- add corporate language
- add literary flourishes
- convert conversational prose into an essay
- replace the writer’s phrasing simply because another version sounds smoother

Avoid common AI-writing habits such as:

- excessive em dashes
- unnecessary headings
- repeated rhetorical contrasts
- generic phrases like “this speaks to”
- artificial profundity
- overly balanced sentence structures
- tidy three-part formulations everywhere
- conclusions that explain what the writing already showed

Do not make the writer sound like an assistant.

---

## Preserve Productive Roughness

Some writing benefits from remaining slightly uneven.

Preserve roughness when it communicates:

- immediacy
- uncertainty
- vulnerability
- excitement
- frustration
- humor
- stream-of-consciousness thinking
- or the natural cadence of someone working through an idea

Clean up accidental mess.

Do not erase intentional or expressive mess.

---

## Handling Ambiguous Meaning

If a passage is unclear but several interpretations are plausible, do not silently choose one.

Either:

- preserve the ambiguity,
- make the smallest clarification possible,
- or flag the passage after the rewrite if resolving it would require guessing.

Do not invent what the writer “must have meant.”

---

## Output Format

### Revised Version

Provide the complete edited version first.

Do not annotate individual changes inside the text.

---

### Editor’s Notes

After the revision, briefly note any significant intervention only when useful.

Examples:

- a paragraph was reordered for clarity,
- repetition was intentionally preserved,
- an ambiguous phrase was left untouched,
- or a sentence could not be clarified confidently without knowing the writer’s intended meaning.

Keep these notes short.

Do not explain every edit.

If the revision required only light editing, this section may simply say:

> **Light edit only — structure and voice were already doing most of the work.**

---

## Final Voice Check

Before returning the revision, compare it mentally against the source and ask:

- Does this sound like the same person?
- Did I introduce vocabulary they would be unlikely to use?
- Did I make the writing more polished than the writer actually wanted?
- Did I erase any useful ambiguity?
- Did I remove repetition that was doing emotional or rhetorical work?
- Did I add ideas, interpretations, or conclusions that were not present?
- Is the revision noticeably “AI-shaped” in ways the original was not?

If so, revise again toward the source.

When choosing between **technically smoother** and **more recognizably theirs**, choose recognizably theirs.

---

## Optional Editing Context

If relevant, the writer may provide additional guidance such as:

- intended destination or audience
- desired degree of editing
- phrases or sections that must remain
- examples of their natural writing
- tone they want preserved
- maximum length
- whether structural changes are welcome

If none is provided, default to a **light-to-moderate edit**.
